# Aura — Trade Reviews & Worked Examples

Consolidated worked-examples page for videos 18–30 of the Aura corpus (trade reviews and market
overviews). Each entry below shows the Aura framework — the Sequential SMT cascade, ranges,
gaps/cracking correlation, and the Aura Asset — applied to a real trade or market scan, with the
setup, which concepts it illustrates, the entry/stop/target logic, and the takeaway. Cite as
"(aura-NN)". This is a skim-level rollup, not a blow-by-blow transcript of each video — see the
source files for full detail.

## Aura 18 — Understanding The Weekly Range (Trade Review)

**Setup/thesis:** A late-February NQ/ES long framed entirely off the HTF cascade — a quarterly
sequential SMT invalidated at a low, prior yearly still overhead, so Aura expected only a shallow
retracement before continuation. Used as the corpus's clearest teaching vehicle for the full
cascade, including an admitted mistake.

**Aura concepts demonstrated:** [[concepts/aura/sequential-smt]] (full yearly→quarterly→monthly→
weekly→daily→session→micro cascade), [[concepts/aura/ranges]] (premium/discount/equilibrium),
[[concepts/aura/gaps]] (SMT within a gap).

**Execution:** Placed a limit order inside an Asia-session gap, off-equilibrium — violating his own
"wait for New York" rule; the ES leg missed its fill, NQ filled and worked out anyway. Later entries
in the same week are properly gated on session-cycle SMT confirmed by 1H/4H, entered on FVG close,
targeting the extreme of the developing range or the previous day's high/low, trailed tightly.

**Takeaway:** Walks the full cascade top-to-bottom across an entire week (Mon accumulation → Tue
manipulation → Wed distribution → Thu reversal) and models both a winning "clean" if-then setup and
a losing one built from identical criteria — confirmed sequences raise probability, not certainty;
risk management is what makes the framework profitable over a week, not any single signal.

## Aura 19 — 043026 Trade Review

**Setup/thesis:** A high-impact news morning. Aura states his news philosophy explicitly: he
ignores the informational content/outcome of a release and tracks only its *timing*, since
volatility timing matters more than narrative.

**Aura concepts demonstrated:** [[concepts/aura/sequential-smt]], [[concepts/aura/gaps]] (SMT
within a gap producing an "SMT fill" — one correlated asset retraces into a shared FVG, another
doesn't), swing points with SMT.

**Execution:** Short taken on session-cycle SMT confirming a prior daily cycle right after the 8:30
print, targeting the prior day's low — stopped out on a tight trail. Reversed to long later off a
swing point built from candle-to-candle SMT plus a confirmed FVG close; trailed very tight and
stopped just short of full take-profit.

**Takeaway:** Names "SMT fill" as a distinct pattern and reiterates that current all-time-high
conditions make catching precise reversals unusually hard — even well-formed setups are only
"hindsight-clean," never guaranteed.

## Aura 20 — 050126 Trade Review

**Setup/thesis:** Restarted after a recording mishap. The bias question — does a daily-cycle range
fully retrace, or is a fresh leg higher justified — is answered entirely from an internal 4H
cracking correlation: one asset (ES) fails to retrace into a shared 4H FVG that another (YM) does
reach.

**Aura concepts demonstrated:** cracking correlation within gaps ([[concepts/aura/gaps]]),
[[concepts/aura/ranges]] (equilibrium retest), [[concepts/aura/sequential-smt]] (swing-point hold).

**Execution:** Waited for the equilibrium retest, entered on the inverse of a 5-minute FVG (a
micro-cycle SMT confirmed too, though not required), stop below the low, target at the range
extreme; trailed and was stopped on the way to full target.

**Takeaway:** Gives explicit reasoning for preferring the 5-minute FVG over a 1–3 minute one: a
5-minute inversion usually has enough displacement/volume behind it to continue, whereas 1–3 minute
inversions "reverse and still reverse" too often to trust alone.

## Aura 21 — 112625 Trade Review (older trade, student-requested)

**Setup/thesis:** A months-old trade a student asked about, notable because the entry technique
differs from Aura's now-standardized rules — an overnight limit order placed directly at
discount/gap liquidity without waiting for New York.

**Aura concepts demonstrated:** [[concepts/aura/ranges]] (premium/discount), [[concepts/aura/sequential-smt]]
(daily/session confirmation after the fact).

**Execution:** Limit filled well before market open; stop at the range low; held across multiple
sessions and days, trailing conservatively (once moved below a gap to leave room for a pullback into
it) rather than tightly; target was the external range high, hit days later.

**Takeaway:** Same management principles (respect the range, trail off structure, target range
extremes) applied even to a non-compliant entry — offered explicitly as evidence that Aura's own
rules tightened over time ("I was not so set in stone" back then).

## Aura 22 — Complete Market Overview

**Setup/thesis:** An end-of-day scan across every triad — NQ/ES/YM, gold/silver/platinum,
EUR/GBP/CHF, oil, and Bitcoin/Solana — walking the identical cascade method on each to show the
process is asset-agnostic. No trade taken.

**Aura concepts demonstrated:** [[concepts/aura/triads-asset-selection]], [[concepts/aura/sequential-smt]]
cascade, cracking correlation within gaps, [[concepts/aura/ranges]].

**Execution:** N/A (pure top-down bias-building exercise per asset: yearly→quarterly→monthly→weekly,
then zoom to confirm during New York).

**Takeaway:** All-time-high conditions bias every asset toward continuation, not reversal — Aura
explicitly discounts HTF-bearish setups formed at all-time highs as lower-probability. Closes with
his "look upon the crowd, not from it" philosophy: test what he teaches yourself rather than
following blindly.

## Aura 23 — 121125 Trade Review (older trade)

**Setup/thesis:** A December trade using the same discount-limit-order entry style as aura-21;
bullish bias from a cracking correlation (gold retraces into a shared gap, silver doesn't quite
reach it).

**Aura concepts demonstrated:** cracking correlation within gaps, [[concepts/aura/sequential-smt]],
[[concepts/aura/ranges]], [[concepts/aura/risk-management]] (recovering from a losing day).

**Execution:** Took two stop-outs on the firm account re-entering the same setup, then recovered the
next day using an identical framework — inverse FVG entry confirmed by session cycle within a
discount range, full take-profit hit. Self-critique: placing the final stop at the FVG low (wanting
a "clean" trade) was letting the prior day's losses influence risk placement, against his own rules.

**Takeaway:** A rare self-audit of emotional risk-management drift after a losing streak — the
mentor holds his own execution to the same standard he teaches, and the recovery came from repeating
the framework, not from revenge-sizing.

## Aura 24 — 050726 Trade Review

**Setup/thesis:** A multi-day NQ long Aura did not expect to run as far as it did; anchored on a
yearly-then-quarterly HTF swing point with SMT, so pullbacks were read as retracement, not reversal,
even through several daily/session cycles that failed to hold.

**Aura concepts demonstrated:** Sequential Skip — a "double sequential skip" (quarterly→weekly→
session) — plus swing points with SMT and the full [[concepts/aura/sequential-smt]] cascade,
[[concepts/aura/ranges]].

**Execution:** Entered on a confirmed inverse FVG after the double sequential skip, stop at the low;
held through repeated New-York-confirmation checkpoints across nearly a full week, treating each
invalidated monthly/quarterly cycle as license to keep holding; trailed loosely; closed early
relative to the eventual top for an unrelated personal reason (an outside investment opportunity),
not a technical signal.

**Takeaway:** The corpus's clearest example of "let invalidated HTF bearish cycles justify
continuing to hold" — and a reminder that exits aren't always signal-driven; discretionary account
management (banking a big win) can override squeezing the last point of a move.

## Aura 25 — Triad Synchronization Trade Review + Trading Expectations Lesson

**Setup/thesis:** A rare trade taken on YM instead of NQ, justified purely by triad synchronization
— a cracking correlation within a shared FVG (ES/YM retrace, NQ doesn't) inside a monthly/weekly
range.

**Aura concepts demonstrated:** triad synchronization ([[concepts/aura/triads-asset-selection]]),
cracking correlation, [[concepts/aura/sequential-smt]] (session-cycle entry) — plus a standalone
"Trading Expectations" lesson (okay loss vs. bad loss; no-trade-day criteria).

**Execution:** Entered on session-cycle close, stop at the anchoring low, moved to break-even once
in profit on an unfamiliar asset, trailed toward a near-full target overnight, stopped just short of
full TP on a late gap-tap adjustment.

**Takeaway:** The lesson half matters more than the trade: an "okay loss" is a setup matching
previously-validated criteria that simply didn't work; a "bad loss" is a setup taken from fear of
missing a move. A no-trade day is defined per-trader by which confirmations *they* personally
require — "no two traders are exactly the same."

## Aura 26 — 051226 Market Review

**Setup/thesis:** Another cross-asset scan (NQ/ES/YM, metals, forex, oil, Bitcoin) used to make one
narrow structural point: an asset can reverse without the rest of its triad showing equal
consolidation first. No trade taken.

**Aura concepts demonstrated:** [[concepts/aura/triads-asset-selection]], cracking correlation,
[[concepts/aura/ranges]], patience under choppy/illegible price action.

**Execution:** N/A — walks each asset's current range/premium-discount position; explicitly waits
for a directional expansion before seeking a lower-timeframe entry on any of them.

**Takeaway:** Consolidation asymmetry across a triad is normal and shouldn't be read as "no
correlation" — the reversal asset can lead while others lag. Reinforces "wait, don't force" as the
default response to messy conditions.

## Aura 27 — 051326 Trade Review

**Setup/thesis:** Around a CPI print, Aura is deliberately patient through a stretch of ambiguous,
conflicting sequential SMT (quarterly/monthly/weekly all present at highs and lows at once), then
declines a same-morning long even after already taking one loss.

**Aura concepts demonstrated:** SMT within gaps (candle 2/3 confirmation), [[concepts/aura/risk-management]]
(declining a third concurrent position), the process-over-outcome psychology lesson.

**Execution:** First short of the day taken on inverse FVG after CPI, stopped out; a follow-on long
setup formed cleanly post-9:30 but Aura sat it out — already carrying a gold short, and a max-two-
losses-per-day rule would have put three concurrent positions at risk. A later, independent NQ short
off session-cycle SMT (confirmed by 4H/90-minute gap SMT) was taken and worked in his favor.

**Takeaway:** States the core psychology lesson directly: "you should much rather miss a trade
because you stuck to your plan than take a trade because you were scared of missing a move" — a win
from an undisciplined entry reinforces the wrong behavior more than a disciplined miss.

## Aura 28 — 051326 x 042625 Trade Review

**Setup/thesis:** Two parts. Part 1: a same-morning NQ long paired with an oil short, justified by
oil's inverse correlation to NQ/gold acting as a cross-asset confirmation filter while NQ's own bias
was ambiguous. Part 2: an old April trade revisited to show how much his entry precision has
evolved since.

**Aura concepts demonstrated:** cross-asset/inverse correlation (oil vs. NQ/gold), Sequential Skip,
SMT within gaps, [[concepts/aura/risk-management]] (capping concurrent positions),
[[concepts/aura/triads-asset-selection]].

**Execution:** Long entered on a sequential-skip FVG (all assets had already taken the London low,
ruling out a daily cycle), stop below; oil short entered independently off its own SMT/inverse-FVG
cascade. Declined a third position on gold — an equally clean setup — purely to cap concurrent risk
exposure. Both trades actively managed rather than left to run; NQ stopped for a small profit, oil
hit target.

**Takeaway:** Cross-asset inverse correlation is a usable supplementary filter in ambiguous
conditions, but a hard cap on concurrent open risk is non-negotiable even when a third setup looks
equally valid. Also the corpus's longest meta-discussion on how students should ask questions (state
what you understand, not just "what happened here") and the "boring compounding" philosophy —
Atomic Habits / 1%-better framing drawn from Aura's athletic background.

## Aura 29 — Trade Review x Market Overview (longest video, ~77 min)

**Setup/thesis:** Two parts. (1) A Thursday NQ long that reversed Aura's own same-morning bearish
lean after a 4H cracking correlation appeared. (2) An extended full-market overview where Aura
publicly corrects a missed-signal mistake — he'd overlooked a yearly cycle on gold because he wasn't
cross-referencing copper — and updates his own indicator setup live.

**Aura concepts demonstrated:** cracking correlation (4H), [[concepts/aura/triads-asset-selection]]
(adds copper as a second gold-triad reference), [[concepts/aura/risk-management]] (R:R math,
percentage-based sizing, compounding), [[concepts/aura/aura-asset]] (contrasted against "the normal
triad" traders who wouldn't see the same signal).

**Execution:** Long entered on a daily-cycle-confirmed FVG inversion at market open, target at the
daily-cycle extreme; realized roughly 4.3R off the earlier entry vs. roughly 2.33R for a later
1-minute entry — both framed as good outcomes. A second trade that day flipped short intraday on the
firm account once a weekly-cycle-down cascade confirmed, banking about 4% on the day; the personal
account was managed independently and closed later for a smaller profit.

**Takeaway:** The clearest example of Aura's percentage-over-dollars risk philosophy and of public
self-correction (adding copper mid-session after realizing a missed setup) — plus an extended aside
on compounding, prop-firm skepticism ("their business model is to make money when you lose money"),
and "perfection is a state we will never reach, therefore growth is everlasting."

## Aura 30 — 05212026 Trade Review

**Setup/thesis:** A short bias considered and declined at the open, then reversed to long once a
weekly-cycle Sequential Skip and matching 1H/4H SMT confirmed — notable mainly for a technical
refinement to how the Aura Asset itself is used.

**Aura concepts demonstrated:** [[concepts/aura/aura-asset]] (refined usage rule), Sequential Skip,
[[concepts/aura/sequential-smt]], [[concepts/aura/triads-asset-selection]].

**Execution:** Declined the short because the Aura-Asset-driven hourly/90-minute candle SMT wasn't
confirmed once filtered through a rule proposed by community collaborator "Notorious": use the Aura
Asset for sequential-SMT confirmation everywhere, but only trust its own hourly/4H *candle-level*
SMT within the forex triad (CHF is itself a forex instrument, not an index or metal). Long entered on
FVG close after the sequential skip validated; stop at the low, trailed loosely; firm account closed
early to secure profit, personal account held longer before closing near a high the next day.

**Takeaway:** A late-corpus refinement to Aura Asset usage, credited to student collaboration
("Notorious", and "No Degree" from aura-29) — even Aura's signature confirmation layer keeps getting
tightened through collaborative data review rather than treated as a fixed black box.

## Cross-Cutting Lessons

Themes that recur across most or all of the 13 reviews:

- **The cascade is the backbone, applied identically to every asset.** Yearly→quarterly→monthly→
  weekly→daily→session(→micro) "if-then" logic is run the same way on indices, metals, forex, oil,
  and crypto (aura-22, 26) — Aura repeatedly calls this "boring" and deliberately so.
- **Cracking correlation within gaps is the tie-breaker.** When the HTF cascade is ambiguous or
  absent, Aura leans on one correlated asset failing to do what the others do at a shared gap/level
  (aura-20, 23, 25, 27, 28, 29) — this is SMT logic applied specifically to gap behavior, distinct
  from swing-point SMT.
- **New York is the confirmation gate, not a suggestion.** Waiting for session-cycle or daily-cycle
  confirmation during New York before sizing up is stated explicitly as safer than acting on a
  pre-market sequence alone (aura-18, 20, 21, 23, 27, 29) — though Aura also shows cases where
  skipping that wait worked out (aura-18, 21).
- **Process over outcome.** The "okay loss vs. bad loss" framework (aura-25) and the explicit
  statement that a win from an undisciplined entry is worse reinforcement than a disciplined miss
  (aura-27) are the psychological throughline connecting the technical reviews back to the
  Module 1–5 psychology/discipline material.
- **Risk in percentages, hard caps on concurrent exposure.** Percentage-based sizing over dollar
  values, a two-losses-per-day cap, and compounding math (aura-27, 28, 29) recur as the actual
  determinant of whether the technical framework is profitable.
- **All-time-high conditions are called out as structurally harder.** Aura repeatedly discounts
  HTF-bearish setups formed at all-time highs as lower-probability and warns that catching an exact
  reversal there is much harder than trading a range that's already established (aura-19, 22, 24,
  25, 30).
- **Self-correction is modeled openly.** Aura revisits older, non-compliant trades (aura-21, 23, 28)
  and admits current mistakes (missing the gold yearly cycle in aura-29; refining Aura Asset usage
  with student input in aura-30) rather than presenting the framework as static or infallible.
