# Psychology Foundations

dOoMeR opens the Aura mentorship with a diagnosis, not a strategy: trading results are broken because
the *inputs* — discipline, emotional control, habits, environment, honest self-assessment — are broken,
and no strategy can survive broken inputs. The entire first week is deliberately non-technical: "we're
not touching a chart. We're fixing the person who sits in front of the chart" (aura-01).

## The Survey: Diagnosing 142 Students

Before the mentorship launched, dOoMeR surveyed 142 prospective students on their trading, habits,
struggles, and life outside the charts. The results, cited directly (aura-01):

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Break-even or losing traders | 67% |
| Have blown at least one account | 95% |
| Journal consistently | only 34% |
| Rate stress management as fair/poor | 54% |
| Spend 4+ hours/day on phone outside trading | 41% (self-reported — he believes real number is higher, closer to 6-7 hrs) |
| Trading experience | 6-9% have traded 1-5 years (transcript figure as captioned; see note below) |

> **Transcription note:** the line "6 and 9% have been trading 1 to 5 years" is very likely a
> mis-caption of "69% have been trading 1 to 5 years" — auto-caption splitting a two-digit percentage
> across "and." Flagged rather than silently corrected in the table above since the original number
> isn't recoverable with certainty from context alone.

dOoMeR's read of this data: most students have been at it for years, aren't profitable, have blown
accounts, don't journal, are stressed, and burn hours daily on their phones. "That's not a strategy.
That's a foundation problem."

## The Misdiagnosis

When asked what's keeping them from consistent profitability, the most common survey answer was
**"finding a consistent strategy."** dOoMeR calls this a misdiagnosis: the same respondents report they
can't follow the rules they already have, can't sit on their hands, revenge trade, overtrade, cut
winners, and hold losers. "The strategy isn't the barrier. You are." He states explicitly that he
doesn't care which strategy a student uses — "there's no elitism here" — his aim is to make students
execute *whatever* strategy they already have (aura-01).

## The Repeating Loop

dOoMeR frames trader failure as a cycle, not a one-time mistake:

> Learn something new → feel motivated → try it → hit resistance → break rules → lose money → feel
> like a failure → search for a new strategy → repeat.

His claim: **new information does not break this loop.** "Changing the person running the system
will." (aura-01)

## Four Primary Trading Fears (Mark Douglas)

Citing Mark Douglas's *Trading in the Zone*: 95% of trading errors trace back to attitudes about
**being wrong, losing money, missing out, and leaving money on the table** — the four primary trading
fears. dOoMeR ties the survey results directly to this: emotional discipline, FOMO, revenge trading,
and overtrading are not strategy failures, they are **human failures** — none of the respondents have
"overtrading" or "revenge trading" written into their actual strategy, yet they do it anyway (aura-01).

## The Real Problem: Broken Inputs

Trading results are an *output*. The inputs are discipline, emotional control, habits, environment, and
the ability to honestly self-assess. "If the inputs are broken, no strategy on earth will produce
consistent results. You wouldn't pour clean water through a cracked pipe and expect it to come out
clean on the other side." (aura-01)

## The Dopamine Problem

dOoMeR treats phone/social-media use as a direct input into trading performance, not a separate
lifestyle issue:

- Citing Andrew Huberman (Huberman Lab podcast, dopamine and motivation): every scroll, notification
  check, or low-effort content consumption spikes dopamine, followed by a crash below baseline.
  Repeated enough, baseline drops permanently.
- Consequence for trading: this rewires the brain to need constant stimulation while "trading rewards
  the exact opposite" — discipline under boredom.
- Context stats cited: average American ~7 hrs/day screen time, Gen Z 9+ hrs/day, average phone check
  frequency 96x/day ("if I told you to do 100 push-ups every single day, that would seem like a lot" —
  yet 96 checks/day is the norm and goes unquestioned).

## The Lifestyle Problem: Sleep

- 54% of survey respondents rated stress management as fair/poor; many sleep 5-6 hours on irregular
  schedules.
- Citing Huberman: deep sleep is how the dopamine reservoir refills nightly. Starting a trading day on
  a depleted tank means starting without "the neurochemistry to make sharp decisions." Framed as
  biology, not motivational advice: "you can't outperform your physiology."
- dOoMeR discloses he works at a proprietary trading firm on the institutional side (in addition to
  managing personal capital) and says the group of institutional traders he's part of wear sleep
  trackers and **compete with each other over who is most rested** — offered as evidence that sleep,
  recovery, and readiness (not chart patterns) are what top performers actually prioritize. He admits
  his own sleep was historically poor and that improving it produced a noticeable change.

## The Influencer Illusion

Social media creates a false picture that traders can wake minutes before market open and make money
from a phone, or live a party lifestyle like online trading influencers. dOoMeR's position: the
influencer lifestyle is a marketing tool (it drives views/platform growth), not how people who actually
perform consistently live — those people protect their environment and sleep and "treat this like the
profession it is." He allows that occasional fun (e.g., after a good trading week) isn't inherently
harmful — the problem is *constant/consistent* indulgence, not any indulgence.

## Systems Over Goals (James Clear / Atomic Habits)

The mentorship's opening quote, attributed to James Clear (*Atomic Habits*): **"You do not rise to the
level of your goals, you fall to the level of your systems."** dOoMeR illustrates with Clear's British
Cycling Team story: the team was poor for decades until Dave Brailsford took over and changed nothing
about the goal — only the system, auditing everything down to pillows, hand soap, and uniform washing,
improving each element by ~1%. Within a few years the team dominated the Tour de France and Olympics.
His conclusion: **"You don't need a better goal, you need a better system. And the system starts with
you."** (aura-01)

A second illustration, from an unnamed successful institutional trader referred to only as "Tom" in the
transcript (see note): it took him seven years to become profitable because he kept repeating the same
mistake — blowing an account with no stop-loss, then retrying the same way and hoping for a different
result, without changing anything.

> **Note on "Tom":** the transcript's attribution is inconsistent — this quote is introduced as "another
> quote from Th[om]" and, elsewhere in the same video, as being from "Tom" without a surname. Given later
> Aura videos reference "Tom Dante" and "the Institute of Trading" by name, this is almost certainly Tom
> Dante, but video 1 itself never gives the full name — flagged rather than silently filled in.

## The Foundation Week: Required Deliverables

By the end of week one, dOoMeR requires students to produce, in writing:

1. A written self-assessment
2. A real understanding of how their mind and habits sabotage their trading
3. Concrete rules for emotional control
4. A daily routine designed for discipline
5. A journaling system that turns vague problems into specific, fixable ones

He's explicit that none of this happens automatically — "it requires you to do actual work" — and that
everything taught in weeks 2-8 builds on this foundation.

## Homework (Video 1)

1. Write down your top three trading struggles, brutally honest and specific (not "psychology" or
   "discipline" in the abstract — e.g. "I revenge trade after two consecutive losses," "I can't hold a
   winner past one," "I take trades that aren't in my plan because I'm bored").
2. Write down the last three times you broke your own rules — what happened before, during, and after
   each; what triggered it; what the result was. (If you can't even recall three instances, that itself
   signals you aren't tracking your own rule-breaking.)
3. Rate yourself honestly, numerically, on: sleep, diet, stress management, phone discipline. Write the
   numbers down.

Recommended reading/viewing cited in this video: *Atomic Habits* (James Clear), *Trading in the Zone*
(Mark Douglas), *The Four Agreements*, plus Huberman Lab and Tom Anton / Institute of Trading YouTube
content on dopamine, sleep, and motivation.
