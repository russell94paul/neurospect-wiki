# HTF to LTF Application

dOoMeR's applied, top-down workflow for combining [[concepts/aura/aura-asset]] and Sequential SMT
divergence signals across timeframes, framed as a cascading **if-then decision chain** modeled on
institutional/algorithmic logic, and demonstrated through two full worked trade reviews (aura-17).
This is presented as the payoff lesson that ties every prior technical concept together — "everything
that I've given you guys when I have given it to you is for a reason" (aura-16) — rather than
introducing new mechanics.

## The If-Then Framework (Why)

dOoMeR opens by explaining *why* he structures analysis as if-then logic rather than intuition: he
frames institutional trading firms' preference for algorithms as a way to remove human/emotional error
— "there is no emotion within an algorithm... for the most part it is an if-then statement" (aura-17).
He is explicit that he isn't claiming algorithmic expertise himself; he's borrowing the *logical
structure* as a discipline tool — "a great way to eliminate your impulses and a great way to notice
when you have impulses is to think like a machine" (aura-17). This ties directly back to the
mentorship's psychology-first framing (see [[concepts/aura/psychology-foundations]]).

## The Cascade (Step by Step)

The same if-then logic applies at every timeframe pair; dOoMeR walks it from the highest timeframe
down (aura-17):

| If... | ...then look for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Quadrennial cycle | Yearly **or** Quarterly | Quadrennial can skip straight to Quarterly, bypassing Yearly — the same "skip" mechanic as Sequential Skip (see [[concepts/aura/sequential-smt]]) |
| Yearly | Quarterly **or** Monthly | |
| Quarterly | Monthly **or** Weekly | |
| Monthly | Daily **or** Weekly | dOoMeR prefers confirming Monthly with **Weekly** first — the higher low of the week tends to be driven by weekly-cycle Sequential SMT, which is why he keeps emphasizing "playing the weekly range" |
| Weekly | Daily **or** Session cycle | Confirmed two ways: (a) the current day's price action forms a reversal, or (b) the New York session confirms — NY session volume/order flow can confirm *or reverse* a higher-timeframe bias |
| Daily / Session cycle | 4H SMT | |
| 4H / entry timeframe | 15m → 5m (preferred) or 3m | 5m preferred over 3m because 3m setups more often still fail even when present |

Target selection follows the same top-down logic: target the **extreme of the timeframe you're
playing** (e.g., previous week's low/high if trading off the monthly/weekly). dOoMeR is explicit that
price will not usually run cleanly to target — "price will not fall directly to your target because as
it's falling, it's developing more ranges and more sequential [SMT]" — so trailing the stop and active
management is expected, not a sign the model failed.

## Worked Trade Review 1 — Bullish HTF Continuation

dOoMeR walks a live example of the cascade in practice (aura-17):

1. Quadrennial cycle and Yearly high already identified going into the trade.
2. Cascading down: Quarterly, then Monthly, then Weekly all lined up in the same direction.
3. As the new day opened, he ideally waits for the New York session to confirm the weekly bias — but
   entered early, during the Asia session, on a **retest of the daily-cycle Sequential SMT's inverse
   FVG**. He is candid about the risk taken: "I did get lucky on this trade... I was very close to
   being stopped out." His justification for not waiting for another weekly confirmation: there was
   already so much weekly-cycle stacking above, supported by the daily cycle within the NY session
   timeframe, that an additional weekly confirmation wasn't needed.
4. **Stop placement:** at the high of the daily-cycle SMT / 4H SMT — i.e., the level whose respect
   would invalidate the trade. "I did not want to see that get respected. So if it got respected, that
   would be my invalidation and I place my stop loss there."
5. **Alternate-asset entry technique:** when a primary asset's stop distance is uncomfortably large,
   the same signal can be taken on a correlated triad member with a tighter equivalent gap instead —
   in this example, entering on YM rather than ES for a materially smaller stop on the same underlying
   idea. This reuses a technique from an earlier lesson on alternate-asset entries.
6. **Target:** the extreme of the higher timeframe being played — the previous week's low.
7. **Outcome:** the trade took a long time to play out and price did not fall directly to target; it
   required active trailing-stop management as new ranges and Sequential SMT formed along the way.

## Worked Trade Review 2 — Divergence on Both Sides (Chop, Bias Flip)

dOoMeR treats this as the harder case: Sequential SMT / divergence appears on **both** the high and low
side simultaneously (weekly above **and** weekly below), producing consolidation because "the weeklys
on both sides can hold" (aura-17).

1. Weekly-above (bearish) bias held for two consecutive days, confirmed by a 4H SMT and a daily-cycle
   confirmation. Going into market open, the if-then chain calls for a session-cycle confirmation next.
2. **Discipline checkpoint:** he expected the session cycle to complete with a bearish inverse FVG
   trigger on the 5-minute. It did not appear — instead, a **bullish** 5m FVG formed. He treats the
   absence of the expected trigger as an explicit invalidation signal, not a reason to keep waiting: "if
   that happened... I am zooming out." He notes he checked the 3-minute too but prefers to act off the
   5-minute specifically because 3-minute setups more often still fail even when present.
3. **Bias flip:** zooming back out, a weekly cycle had formed at the low — which flips the bias to
   bullish. He enters on the inverse FVG confirmation at that point.
4. **Stop:** the recent swing low. **Target:** previous day's high — a deliberately simple target while
   "navigating the weekly range."
5. He is explicit that this is the if-then framework working as designed, not a change of mind: he had
   been positioned/expecting the bearish read, the expected trigger failed to appear, and the framework
   itself supplied the next question to ask ("if the weekly supports that, then you can take the
   trade") rather than requiring him to guess.

## Why Higher Timeframes Dominate

dOoMeR's stated reasoning for weighting HTF divergence over LTF divergence: higher-timeframe candles
aggregate more data, volume, and news than lower-timeframe candles, so divergences on those candles
carry more real influence over price. A 1-minute "microcycle" divergence is common but low-influence —
"you're not going to have a massive expansive move just because a microcycle presented itself" (aura-17).

## Homework (Video 17)

Continue applying the if-then cascade across the weekly range, explicitly defining **both** what would
confirm **and** what would invalidate a bias before taking a trade. dOoMeR reiterates the risk-first
framing from [[concepts/aura/risk-management]]: "our goal is not to catch every move. Our goal is to
survive... manage our risk."
