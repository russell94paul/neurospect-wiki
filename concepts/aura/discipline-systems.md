# Discipline Systems (Aura)

dOoMeR's core claim in video 4 is that discipline is not a willpower problem, it's a design problem: willpower is a depleting resource that runs out fastest on exactly the days trading discipline matters most (tired, stressed, post-loss, distracted), so the fix is to build routines and environment that make the disciplined choice the default rather than a decision you have to win in the moment (aura-04).

## Core Thesis — Systems Over Willpower

- "You do not rise to the level of your goals, you fall to the level of your systems" (James Clear, *Atomic Habits*, quoted directly). If a system requires making the right decision in the heat of the moment, it's a bad system (aura-04).
- A good system makes the disciplined choice the default — the easiest option, the one that happens when you're not thinking about it (aura-04).
- Grounding reference: Napoleon Hill's 1954 concept of "cosmic habit force" — once a habit is established it runs automatically, for you or against you. dOoMeR frames this as: deliberately install habits that serve you, deliberately remove the ones that don't (aura-04).
- Motivation/environment framing (via James Clear): most daily actions are shaped by the most obvious option available, not by purposeful choice. "If you want to make a habit a big part of your life, make the cue a big part of your environment" — and the inverse: make bad behavior harder to access and good behavior the path of least resistance (aura-04).

## The Pre-Market System

What you do before the open determines how you trade the session — improvising at the open means you've already lost (aura-04). Illustrative example: a trader at Tom Dante's prop firm who consistently arrived just before the open, never had time to plan, and lost his first one or two trades almost every day — not a strategy problem, a preparation problem (aura-04).

**Before every session, you must be able to answer three questions.** If you can't answer all three, you're not ready to trade (aura-04):
1. What am I looking for?
2. Where am I looking for it?
3. What would cause me to stand aside?

**Pre-market routine components:**
- Wake at the same time every day.
- No phone for the first 30–60 minutes after waking.
- Morning sunlight + movement before any screen input (sets the day's dopamine baseline).
- Review your levels — mark key areas on the charts.
- Write your plan for the day before price starts moving.

## The Post-Market System

The post-market routine is how you grow: review what happened, journal your trades, note what you did right and what you broke (aura-04). See [[concepts/aura/journaling-system]] for the journaling method itself — this page covers the *scheduling discipline* around it.

- Separate the **review** from the **planning** — don't collapse them into one pass. Example cadence from Tom Dante: review on Saturday morning (while the week is still fresh), plan for the week ahead on Sunday evening. dOoMeR is explicit this exact schedule isn't mandatory — the principle (separate + do both consistently) is what matters (aura-04).
- If you miss a session's review, that's acceptable — either do it late or make sure you don't miss the next one. "Your most important decision is the next one" — a phrase dOoMeR repeats across sleep, review, and habit-slip contexts in this video as the recovery principle (aura-04).

**The daily check** — end of every session, answer three questions in about 5 minutes (aura-04):
1. Did I follow my plan?
2. If not, what happened?
3. What is one thing I can do better tomorrow?

dOoMeR frames skipping even this 5-minute check as a seriousness signal ("If [you] can't spend five minutes on self-reflection after a trading session, you're not serious" — auto-caption rendered this as "the cat spends 5 minutes," almost certainly a mis-hearing of "if you can't spend," corrected here) (aura-04).

## Environment Design

"Remove distractions before they remove your edge." Your trading environment should look like a workplace, not an entertainment center (aura-04). Concrete rules given:

- Phone in another room, or on airplane mode, during sessions.
- Close Discord, close Twitter/X, close Telegram.
- No music with lyrics, no YouTube playing in the background.
- Log out of Discord (or other chat apps) on the trading computer specifically, even if you keep it elsewhere.

dOoMeR softens the strictness slightly: if being in a trading Discord/Telegram group or listening to music genuinely helps you focus, that's acceptable — the goal is an environment that reflects and reinforces the mental state you want, not a rigid checklist for its own sake. The failure mode he's targeting is 10 open tabs, multiple chat conversations, and half-attended background music all fragmenting focus at once (aura-04).

## Phone Protocol

Illustrative case: a professional trader ("Anton Creole" in the auto-caption — likely a mis-hearing, name uncertain, see jargon note below) ditched his smartphone and says he became the most efficient he's ever been in his career, after noticing pings every 2–3 minutes for low-value notifications. Cost framing given: at $100/hr valuation, one wasted hour/day of low-value phone use costs $36,500/year; at $1,000/hr, $365,000/year. dOoMeR flags this as a deliberately extreme example, not literally what he expects every trader to model — he does not recommend giving up the phone entirely, only building rules around it (aura-04).

**Concrete phone rules:**
- No phone for the first hour of the day.
- Phone in another room during trading sessions.
- Screen-time limits on social media apps.
- No scrolling before bed.

## Sleep & Physical Health — "The Non-Negotiable"

Framed explicitly as performance optimization, not wellness advice: "you cannot make sharp decisions on five hours of sleep and energy drinks" (aura-04).

**The floor (not aspirational — described as minimums the pros he knows hold themselves to):**
- 7 hours of sleep.
- Consistent wake time every day, including weekends, within 30 minutes.
- Some form of movement every day — doesn't require a gym; a 20-minute walk, push-ups, or stretching between sessions all count.

**Three protocol items said to move the needle on sleep quality, all free:**
1. **Morning sunlight** — get sunlight in your eyes within 30–60 minutes of waking; sets circadian rhythm for the following night. dOoMeR's own routine: wake and either work out or go for a walk outside, which satisfies both the sunlight requirement and the daily-movement requirement in one block.
2. **Caffeine cutoff** — cut caffeine by early afternoon. Reasoning given: caffeine's half-life is roughly 6 hours, so a 2pm coffee is still ~25% active at 2am. Rule of thumb: last coffee before noon (local time) if trading the New York session.
3. **Dim lights after sunset** — bright light at night suppresses melatonin and next-day dopamine release. dOoMeR notes he was initially skeptical this would matter but reports a real subjective effect after a few weeks of consistency. Practical fix for phone use at night: set the phone screen to a full red filter (removes blue light) rather than trying to eliminate pre-bed phone use outright, since he considers that unrealistic for most people.

Attribution: sleep/dopamine claims are attributed throughout to a podcast source rendered as "Human" in the auto-caption — near-certainly **Huberman** (Andrew Huberman / Huberman Lab), corrected throughout this page. See jargon note below.

## Homework (as given in aura-04)

1. Write out your current daily routine as it actually is (not aspirational) — wake to sleep, honestly, including scrolling, late nights, skipped meals.
2. Write a pre-market and post-market checklist: wake time, what you do before charts, what you review after the session.
3. Start following that checklist the next day.
4. Pick two environment changes and implement them immediately (e.g., phone off the desk during sessions, a social-media app blocker, desk cleanup, logging out of Discord on the trading computer).
