# Journaling System (Aura)

dOoMeR's video 5 lays out the philosophy, field set, and review cadence behind his journaling method, summarized in one line attributed to "the most consistent trader" at Tom Dante's prop firm: "keep the ones that work and do the opposite of the ones that don't" (aura-05). The video is light on schema specifics (a fixed template is promised but explicitly deferred to later in the mentorship — see Uncertainties below), but it is detailed and directive about *what categories of data* must be captured and *how* the review process turns that data into behavior change. This page is written for direct use as journal-app product input.

## Core Philosophy

- **Recording ≠ analyzing.** Dante's framing, quoted directly: "There is a huge difference between recording and analyzing your trades." A record you never revisit is worthless — "you might as well not have one." The journal exists for one reason: to make you a better trader (more money if winning, a turnaround if losing) (aura-05).
- **The journal is a habit-change tool, not a diary.** Habit tracking works because it makes a behavior obvious, attractive, and satisfying simultaneously. dOoMeR (citing Huberman — auto-caption rendered "Human," see jargon note) argues you should learn to spike dopamine from the *effort* of following process, not only from outcome/P&L — "the person who falls in love with the journey will get farther than the person who has fallen in love with the destination." Reviewing regularly and finding a pattern is itself framed as the dopamine-triggering event, which is why review cadence (below) is treated as non-negotiable rather than optional polish (aura-05).
- **Journal = accurate thinking.** Reference to Napoleon Hill's 1954 concept of "accurate thinking": first separate facts from fiction, then separate important facts (anything that helps you reach your major purpose) from noise. Without a journal, decisions are made on feelings, recency bias, and guesswork; with one, on data. "That is the difference between a trader and a gambler" (aura-05).
- **Marginal gains framing.** Analogy to the British Cycling Team (the *Atomic Habits* "1% better" case study): they didn't make one big change, they collected data on everything and improved each piece incrementally. The journal is positioned as your equivalent data-collection system (aura-05).

## Why People Quit Journaling

From a survey dOoMeR ran, 50% of respondents don't journal consistently. The stated reasons fall into three buckets, and he frames all three as **instruction problems, not motivation problems** (aura-05):
1. "I don't know what to track."
2. "I don't see the value."
3. "I don't know what to learn from it."

This is the direct justification for the field list and the three-question review framework below — the video is explicitly designed to close all three gaps at once.

## What to Track — The Essentials (baseline, every trade)

Described as the minimum; "most will stop here, but you won't" (aura-05):
- Date
- Session / time
- Setup (the trade type/pattern)
- Entry price
- Exit price
- Stop-loss
- Target
- Risk-to-reward ratio
- Result in R
- Screenshot (dOoMeR notes several of the above can be captured within the screenshot itself rather than as separate fields)

## Psychological Data (per trade) — "Where the Real Information Lives"

dOoMeR is explicit that the numeric fields tell you *what* happened; this layer tells you *why* (aura-05):
- Emotional state **before** the trade
- Emotional state **during** the trade
- Emotional state **after** the trade
- Did you follow your rules? If not, why?
- Was this trade in your plan? If not, what triggered you to take it?

## Missed Trades ("Mist Trades")

The auto-caption renders this repeatedly as "mist trades" / "mistrades" — corrected here to **missed trades**, i.e., trades not taken or not carried through, based on the surrounding description. This category is called out as a distinct, high-value log, separate from executed winners/losers (aura-05):
- Log trades you almost took but didn't.
- Log trades you should have taken but hesitated on.
- Count how many of these would have been losers alongside the winners (don't just log the ones that would have made you feel good in hindsight).

Concrete example given: Dante discovered one of his biggest edges only because someone else was tracking his *canceled* trades — he was consistently pulling orders when the market went vertical into his levels, and didn't realize he was doing it until a student pointed it out with data. dOoMeR's framing: this data is "visible to you right now and it might contain your biggest breakthrough" (aura-05).

## Review Cadence

Both daily and weekly reviews are recommended; dOoMeR does not rank one as primary — the weekly review is described as an accumulation/overview of the daily ones, not a replacement (aura-05).

**Weekly review — process:** once a week, sit down with the journal (his own tool is Notion) and answer, honestly and in detail (aura-05):
- What worked this week? What didn't?
- Was there a type of trade that performed particularly well?
- Were you taking profits too early?
- Were your stop-losses too tight or too wide?
- Do you let winners turn into losers?
- Do you have good ideas but not pull the trigger?
- Are there distractions you need to cut out?

**Active management vs. walking away** — called out as one of the most important comparisons to analyze: track what happened when you actively managed a trade versus what would have happened if you'd set stop/target and walked away. If the data shows you make *less* money by managing, that's "your personal holy grail" — you can make more by doing less work (aura-05).

## The Three-Question Framework (Dante's Questions)

Every review should be oriented around at least one of these three questions, in dOoMeR's stated order of preference — generating more winning ideas first (aura-05):
1. How can I make more money from my winners?
2. How can I lose less money from my losers?
3. How can I generate more winning ideas?

Worked example given for Q1: a trader enters a trade without a specific target/exit in mind, gets scared of losing, moves the stop to breakeven too early, gets stopped out, and then watches price run to the level they were originally watching. The corrective answer under this framework: identify the exit and the reason for it *before* the trade, and accept the loss rather than moving to breakeven prematurely — because that's part of the system, not a failure of nerve (aura-05).

Worked examples given for Q2/Q3: fewer trades via trading a weekly/monthly range instead of day trading (dOoMeR's own stated personal-account approach — "I'm rarely day trading on there"), or simply being more selective; generating more winning ideas comes from being more selective and continuously gathering more data (aura-05).

## Extracting Patterns — Concrete Examples Given

dOoMeR frames these as illustrations of what a working journal produces, not universal rules to copy verbatim (aura-05):
- "If 80% of your losses come from taking trades after 11:00am, you now have data telling you to stop trading after 11:00am. That's not a feeling, that's evidence."
- If your first trade of the day is almost always a loser, that may indicate a need for more preparation time before the open.
- If sizing up correlates with losses (auto-caption: "size up on cup filling" — unclear which term this mis-hears, possibly "conviction" or "confirmation"; left unresolved, see Uncertainties), the implied rule is to keep risk sized fixed rather than scaling in on a feeling.
- dOoMeR's own accumulated-data conclusion (stated as evidence, not a rule for the student to blindly adopt): for day trading, wait for the 9:30am New York open plus 5–10 minutes before taking a trade — he says this pattern became clear only through consistent tracking and review over time.

## Never Miss Twice

Consistency is explicitly prioritized over perfection: "the person who journals 80% of their trades over 6 months will learn infinitely more than the person who journals perfectly for 2 weeks and then stops." The stated rule: missing one entry is an accident; missing twice in a row is the start of a new (bad) habit. If you skip a day, journal the next day no matter what (aura-05).

## Homework (as given in aura-05)

1. Set up the journal using the provided template (see Uncertainties — template contents not given in this video) — "no excuses, no customizing it," use it as-is for the first two weeks before adjusting.
2. Journal every trade from this point forward — winners, losers, and missed trades — for the rest of the mentorship and, implicitly, for the rest of your trading life.
3. Do the first weekly review at the end of the week: review all five days of psychology homework (videos 1–5), identify one takeaway and one behavior change; do the same for trading data; answer the three Dante questions honestly based on current trading.

## Uncertainties / Jargon Corrections

- **No concrete template given in this video.** dOoMeR repeatedly says a template will be "provided separately" / shared later in the mentorship and instructs students not to customize it for the first two weeks. This transcript is the *philosophy and field-category* layer; the literal field list above (essentials + psychological data + missed trades) is the closest thing to a schema this video provides. If a later Aura video (18+, the trade-review sessions) surfaces the actual template in use, that should be reconciled back into this page.
- **"Human" → Huberman.** High-confidence correction, consistent with the same attribution pattern in [[concepts/aura/discipline-systems]] (aura-04) — sleep/dopamine claims via podcast.
- **"Mist trades" / "mistrades" → missed trades.** Corrected throughout based on context (trades almost taken, hesitated on, or canceled) — confident correction, but note the literal caption term in case a future video uses "mistrade" as an intentional coined term rather than a mishearing.
- **"Cup filling"** — unresolved. Left flagged rather than guessed; could be "conviction," "confirmation," or something else entirely.
- **"Tom Dante's broer"** — same garbled reference as in aura-04; rendered as "prop firm" from context.
- No direct self-contradictions found; the "daily vs. weekly" review guidance is presented as complementary rather than conflicting, so not flagged as a contradiction.

## Product Reconciliation Notes

This maps closely onto the existing Neurospect trade schema and roadmap:
- The essentials list (date, session, setup, entry/exit/stop/target, R:R, result in R, screenshot) already exists as first-class fields in `trades` per [[concepts/architecture/trade-schema]], including phase-tagged screenshots.
- `plan_followed`, `mistake_tags`, and `quality_grade` in the schema directly correspond to "did you follow your rules," the psychological/mistake layer, and self-graded execution quality described here.
- **Missed trades are not currently modeled.** dOoMeR treats this as a distinct, high-value category (his strongest single anecdote — Dante's canceled-order edge — comes from it) and it has no equivalent in the current trade schema, which only models executed trades. This is a gap worth raising against [[concepts/architecture/trade-schema]] / [[processes/distributed-workflow/active/journal-analytics]].
- The three-question review framework (winners/losers/ideas) and the pattern-extraction examples (time-of-day loss clustering, first-trade-of-day performance) are natural fits for the analytics/dashboard layer of [[processes/distributed-workflow/active/journal-analytics]] — they're explicitly the kind of aggregate query the "essentials" + `mistake_tags` fields should support.
- Directly relevant to [[concepts/roadmap/ideas/reduce-journaling-friction]]: dOoMeR's own framing ("recording ≠ analyzing," 50% of surveyed traders quit from not knowing what to track) is independent validation that friction and unclear purpose — not motivation — are the adoption blockers, which supports that roadmap item's premise.
- Directly relevant to [[concepts/roadmap/ideas/mistake-driven-action-items]]: the "never miss twice" rule and the pattern-extraction examples (e.g., 80% of losses after 11am) are close in spirit to the recurring-mistake-tag-to-action-item idea already scoped there — Aura's method is manual/reflective where the roadmap idea is closer to automated, but the underlying signal (recurring tagged pattern → concrete rule change) is the same mechanism.
